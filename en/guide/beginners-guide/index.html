ReBirth beginner's guide: tips to win your first campaign

What is your job?

After a great disaster, central authority has collapsed and an armed group called the Shadow Front is filling the vacuum. You coordinate the International Stabilization Mission: save lives, restore order, win the people's trust and, in the end, hand the province back to its people.

ReBirth has no levels. Years pass on the same map and every decision sticks: districts remember what you did, and everything you build stays on the map.

First step: choose your headquarters

In a new campaign you choose where the Mission's headquarters stands, while the Shadow Front is born in a hidden district. Your units move along the road network, so a central district where roads meet lets them reach every corner quickly.

Four phases

The campaign moves through four phases, each with its own goals:

  • Emergency Response: clear the rubble, keep districts standing, reach your first stability.
  • Reconstruction: bring districts under control, grow services and legitimacy, set up local councils.
  • Development and Local Power: train the local army, make tax income overtake donations, hold a successful election.
  • Handover: draw down foreign troops, leave no district to the insurgents, keep stability and legitimacy high.

The number of foreign units you can keep in the field shrinks as the phases go by. Start training local forces early.

Learn to read the districts

Every district has stability, support, security, services, economy, intelligence, insurgent influence, grievance, corruption and trust in you. Tap a district and the panel shows, factor by factor, what is raising or lowering its support.

Map layers (stability, control, security) show at a glance where you need troops, where a hospital and where jobs.

Trust builds slowly, grievance fades slowly

Finished initiatives build trust. Civilian casualties, harsh measures and broken promises build grievance instead; it fades over years, and until then it makes recruiting easier for the Shadow Front.

Think twice before cracking down on a district: the security you gain today can cost you dearly later.

Spend wisely

At first most of your income comes from international donations, but donor fatigue sets in over time. Build a tax base early with initiatives such as markets, farm cooperatives, microcredit and industry.

Spending above the safe threshold triggers inflation and makes everything more expensive. Corruption also takes its cut of the budget; governance initiatives reduce it.

Initiatives and policies

Initiatives cover infrastructure, health, education, governance, economy and security, and each one appears on the map as a lasting building. Some need prerequisites, some unlock in a later phase.

Policies come in four branches: security, governance, society and economy, and cost political capital and budget. Some policies conflict with each other, so read their effects before you choose.

Forces and air power

Units move along the road network on patrol, assault or guard orders. Besides infantry, mechanized units, gendarmerie, special forces, engineers and local forces, you also have air power:

  • The recon drone is your eye in the sky and exposes hidden cells.
  • The armed drone makes precision strikes on neighboring districts.
  • The attack helicopter brings rapid response and close support.
  • The fighter jet delivers heavy strikes anywhere in the province.

Ambushes and roadside bombs can cost you troops, so be careful on roads with weak security.

Know the Shadow Front

The group works through guerrilla, sabotage, propaganda and terror cells, and changes strategy based on your pressure: it hides, spreads, switches to guerrilla war or land grabs, and sometimes seeks talks.

Cells only show up once intelligence in their district is high enough. A steady troop presence, informant networks and recon drones wear down their cover.

Finding and destroying their headquarters shakes the group. But guns are not the only way: amnesty and compromise at the negotiating table are a path to victory too.

Events: every choice has a price

Aftershocks, protests, donor conferences, hostage crises: the call is yours. Colored hints show the effects of each option; some are risky, and their consequences can surface months later.

How not to lose

The campaign ends if your legitimacy stays at rock bottom for too long, international support runs out, the budget sinks deep into the red or your headquarters falls. Check these every month.

Five maps, five disasters

  • Tarsel: earthquakes and mountains. Retrofit homes against aftershocks and set up tent cities.
  • Kesra Delta: a river that floods every spring. Build levees, pumping stations and stilt houses.
  • Varzan Plateau: drought. Drill wells, build cisterns and run water tankers.
  • Halvarya Fjords: long winters and avalanches. Build avalanche barriers and keep the roads open.
  • Saranis Islands: a volcano and the threat of a giant wave. Build breakwaters and an observatory, and run ferries to the islands.

After the campaign

After victory you can keep playing the same map in peacetime. More waits on the main menu: the seven missions of the Crisis Desk, the Shadow Front mode where you lead the insurgency, the country-ruling mode Statecraft, and Sandbox, where you build your own scenarios.
